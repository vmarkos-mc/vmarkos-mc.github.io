Thesis topics can be classified across four highly relevant and dynamic areas in Computer Science (also, quite broad, one might observe):

* **Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI):** This area addresses the "black box" problem in AI, focusing on user trust, accountability, and meeting emerging regulatory requirements like the EU's AI Act. Topics here explore the trade-off between complexity and interpretability, user-centric and causal explanations, and application-specific research such as developing an LLM Debugger or an AI Ranking Diversification system.
* **Human-Machine Interaction (HMI):** HMI is shifting beyond mere usability to focus on deep, ethical engagement with increasingly intelligent and autonomous systems. Key challenges include ensuring System-State Fidelity, protecting Privacy and Security, promoting Inclusivity and Accessibility, and minimizing Cognitive Load & Error Prevention. Indicative topics include investigating technology accessibility for Marginalised Groups and developing methodologies for maximizing Transparency in Human-LLM Interaction.
* **Computer Science Education (CSE):** In a world fundamentally transformed by AI, CS literacy is now a foundational skill. This area is about ensuring we equip students to be critical consumers and responsible shapers of technology. Research could involve reviewing how School Curricula are adapting to AI, studying how tutors and students utilize AI in Educational Practice, or designing and testing Serious Games for Education.
* **Peer-To-Peer (P2P):** P2P technology represents a fundamental shift to decentralized network architectures, leveraging the collective resources of participants to create resilient and autonomous digital ecosystems. This is the foundational technology for content distribution (like BitTorrent) and blockchain systems. Suggested topics include developing a novel P2P Mesh-Networking protocol for environments with intermittent connectivity or creating a fully decentralized, real-time P2P Content Editor using advanced consistency algorithms like CRDT.

I encourage you to read the document thoroughly, as it provides a brief introduction to each area and an accompanying list of example thesis topics.

## Invitation for Your Own Ideas

While the document offers a strong foundation, you are more than encouraged to also think critically and propose your own original thesis topics within these thematic areas. If you have a compelling idea that relates to Explainable AI, Human-Machine Interaction, CS Education, or Peer-to-Peer networking, I welcome you to draft a brief proposal and discuss it with me. Original topics that are well-defined and feasible for a final-year project are always exciting to explore.
 
Please review the document, consider your interests, and, if interested, be prepared to discuss your ideas in an upcoming session.
